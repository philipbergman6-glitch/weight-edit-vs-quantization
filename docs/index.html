The edit: MIT CSAIL Code and raw results

A fix written straight into the weights

MIT CSAIL had a large model edit a small model's weights directly, with no training, so it stops looping on maths. This page follows that edit through rounding to 8 and 4 bits.

Qwen3.5-0.8B: 873,438,784 weights in 488 tensors. Each square in the scope is 65,536 of them.

Two tensors changed. Nothing else.

Takehi, Teoh, Zhang and Khattab (MIT CSAIL, 2 Nov 2026) had a large model read this model's weights and rewrite part of two tensors: layers 15 and 19, mlp.down_proj, 64 columns each. Their figure: 131,072 weights (0.016%). Measured here, 130,188 actually differ.

Result: far less looping. Looping means the answer ran into the 32,768-token limit, the authors' measure. On 100 maths questions here: 88% of answers for the original model, 31% for the edited one.

Layer 15, down_proj: 1,024 x 3,584 weights

Lit in amber: the change, edited minus original. 64 of 3,584 columns carry it, through every row. It is rank-one: one direction holds 99.999% of the change.

Down to single weights

A window of 64 rows x 256 columns, one cell per weight, around the largest single change in the tensor. Then one row of 32 neighbouring weights: the unit that rounding works on.

The tall amber stem is the edited weight. The hollow ring is where it was before the edit.

Rounding snaps every weight to a grid

8-bit (Q8_0): 256 levels per block of 32 weights. 4-bit (stock Q4_K_M, no importance matrix): 16 levels per block of 32; the edited tensors are stored at q4_K. Whole files average 8.52 and 5.51 bits per weight.

At 4-bit the big edited weight stretches its block's scale: its 31 untouched neighbours land on fewer distinct values, 5 in the edited file, 10 in the original.

The edit survives

Read back from the rounded files and compared with the true edit, its direction holds. Cosine 0.9998 at 8-bit; 0.93 (layer 15) and 0.90 (layer 19) at 4-bit. 1 would be a perfect copy.

Bent a little at 4-bit, but there.

Now the answers

100 MATH-500 questions, one answer each, same settings for every version. At 16-bit the edited model gets 61 right.

The knob under the scope turns the precision. Scroll turns it for you, or take it.

8-bit: 45 right

Looping stays low: 43%, against 85% for the original model at 8-bit. But 16 answers were lost, and 14 of them ran into the token limit: answers got longer, median 22,643 to 28,400 tokens.

4-bit: 42 right. The edit is still there.

Looping 29%, against 84% for the original model. This time 12 of the 21 lost answers finished without the right answer.

The fix survived the rounding. The right answers did not.

So the fix came through. Now the answers.

Two questions, two different answers

Did the fix survive?
Yes. Looping 31% to 43% to 29%; original model 84 to 88%.
Is the model still as good?
No. Correct 61 to 45 to 42 of 100.

The prediction was committed before the first run, and it was half wrong: it said 4-bit would erase the edit. It did not. Accuracy fell instead. Why it falls is not measured here.

Limits: 100 questions, one answer each (intervals about plus or minus 10 points), one sampler, one model, one edit.

The edit: Takehi, Teoh, Zhang, Khattab (MIT CSAIL), "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming", 2 Nov 2026. The idea and the edit are theirs; this repo only measures it.
