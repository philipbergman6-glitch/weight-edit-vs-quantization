weight-edit-vs-quantization The edit: MIT CSAIL

A weight edit survives compression. Accuracy drops.

Researchers at MIT CSAIL had a large AI edit a small AI's weights directly, so it gets stuck in loops far less. Small models are usually compressed before people run them. So the edited model was compressed to 8-bit and 4-bit and tested again, on the same 100 maths questions.

An answer that never ends

Qwen3.5-0.8B is a small open model. Like other reasoning models it writes out its thinking before it answers, and it often gets stuck: it finds the answer, then keeps going. Here is the end of one real answer.

Over 100 questions, the original model ran into the limit on 88% of its answers. The edited model: 31%.

Looping, on this page, means one thing: the answer hit the 32,768-token limit. It is the authors' measure. A token is a piece of a word.

The fix: one thin edit, written by another AI

A model's weights are the numbers it is made of: 873,438,784 of them in this one. Normally you change them by training. Takehi, Teoh, Zhang and Khattab had a large model read these numbers and write a change into them directly: no training data, no retraining.

The change is tiny. It sits in 2 of the model's 24 layers, in 64 columns of one matrix in each: 131,072 numbers by the authors' count, 0.016% of the model.

Measured here, 130,188 numbers actually differ between the two files. In each edited matrix, a single direction carries 99.999% of the change.

What compression does

To run on a laptop or a phone, a model is usually compressed. Every number is stored with fewer bits, so it has to be rounded. The original file uses 16 bits per number. At 8-bit, each group of 32 neighbouring numbers can use 256 different values. At 4-bit, only 16.

Whole files average 8.52 and 5.51 bits per number, because some parts are kept at higher precision. The 4-bit file is the stock Q4_K_M recipe, with no importance matrix.

Does the edit survive? Do the answers?

The test: the same 100 questions, same settings, one answer each, for the original and the edited model at 16, 8 and 4 bits. Turn the precision below.

The edit survives. Read back from the files, it keeps a similarity of 0.9998 at 8-bit, and 0.93 (layer 15) and 0.90 (layer 19) at 4-bit. It still does its job: looping stays at 43% and 29%, against 85% and 84% for the original model at the same precision.

The right answers don't. They fall from 61 to 45 to 42. Still far above the original model (14 to 16), but well below the 61 it got uncompressed.

The two levels lose answers in different ways. At 8-bit, 16 answers were lost and 14 of them ran into the token limit: answers got longer, from a median of 22,643 tokens to 28,400. At 4-bit, 21 were lost (and 2 gained); 12 of the lost ones finished on their own, with a wrong or missing answer.

What this does and does not show

It shows that this edit, on this model, still cuts looping after stock 8-bit and 4-bit compression, while the share of right answers falls. It does not show why accuracy falls; that was not measured. One model, one edit, one sampler, 100 questions.

A prediction was committed before the first run. It was half wrong: it said 4-bit would erase the edit. It did not.
